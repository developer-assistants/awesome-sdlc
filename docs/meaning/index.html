SDLC meaning: what is the software development life cycle?

SDLC is the structured process engineering teams follow to plan, design, build, test, deploy, and maintain software systems.

Plain definition

The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) breaks software delivery into ordered stages with clear inputs, outputs, and quality checks. Instead of writing code immediately after an idea appears, a team defines the problem, agrees on requirements, designs the system, implements and tests the code, ships to production, and maintains it over time.

Every software team follows some form of SDLC, whether they write down formal specifications for a regulated bank or run two-week sprints at a ten-person startup. The difference between teams is how much time they spend in each stage and how often they loop back.

Why SDLC exists

Fixing a requirements mistake after launch typically costs 10x to 100x more than catching it during planning or design. A defined lifecycle gives product managers, developers, QA engineers, and operations staff shared checkpoints so defects, security flaws, and scope creep surface before users hit them.

SDLC vs STLC vs PDLC

Three related acronyms often get mixed up in interviews and documentation. SDLC covers the entire engineering lifecycle of the software. STLC (Software Testing Life Cycle) zooms into the testing stage: test planning, test case creation, environment setup, execution, and defect closure. PDLC (Product Development Life Cycle) sits one level wider and includes market research, pricing, launch marketing, and sunsetting the product.
